Learn more

A fat molecule is a triglyceride. Three tails are held on by ester links. Lye cuts those links.

In this model, animal fat is glyceryl tristearate. Olive oil has bent oleic tails, so the soap is softer. Coconut oil has shorter lauric tails, so it makes more bubbles.

NaOH makes bar soap. KOH makes liquid soap. After the reaction, salt can pull the soap out of the glycerol water. That is called salting out. A bar then cures: it dries and hardens.

Key ideas

  • Fat + lye → soap + glycerol. This reaction is called saponification. See the pot
  • Bar or liquid. NaOH makes bar soap. KOH makes liquid soap.
  • Lye amount matters. Too little leaves greasy soap. Too much leaves lye, which can burn.
  • Two ends. Each soap molecule has a water-loving head and a water-fearing tail. See it clean
