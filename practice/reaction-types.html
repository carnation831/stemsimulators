Counting and the five types

How to count

Count each formula once. The big number in front is a coefficient. It does not add a substance. 2H₂ + O₂ is 2 reactants. 3CO₂ + 4H₂O is 2 products. A count of 2 and 2 can be single replacement, double replacement, or combustion, so look at the substances too.

Five patterns

  • Synthesis: substances combine into one product. Usually 2 reactants and 1 product.
  • Decomposition: one reactant breaks into 2 or more products.
  • Single replacement: one element takes the place of another element.
  • Double replacement: two compounds swap partners.
  • Combustion: a carbon compound burns in O₂ and makes CO₂ and H₂O.

Level 3 product states

  • (s) solid metals, ionic solids with no water, and precipitates such as BaSO₄, CaSO₄, and Al(OH)₃.
  • (l) water in this course, and elemental bromine, Br₂.
  • (g) CO₂, NH₃, and the diatomic gases H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, and Cl₂.
  • (aq) dissolved Group 1 salts, nitrates, soluble sulfates, and acids in water such as H₃PO₄.

Hydrogen plus oxygen making only water is synthesis on this site, not combustion. Level 1 has no polyatomic ions. Levels 2 and 3 include ions such as nitrate, sulfate, hydroxide, carbonate, phosphate, ammonium, chlorate, and hydrogen carbonate.

How an answer works

Count each substance once. Ignore coefficients. In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O there are 2 reactants and 1 product.

Then choose synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement, or combustion.

Level 3 also asks for the state of each product. Those states are left off the equation. Choose (s), (l), (g), or (aq).
