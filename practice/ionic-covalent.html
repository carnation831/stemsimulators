How to tell ionic from covalent

Metal or nonmetal

Metals in this set: Li, Na, K, Mg, Ca, Ba, Al, Zn, Fe, Cu, Ag.

Nonmetals: H, C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl, Br, I. Hydrogen is a nonmetal. This practice does not use metalloids.

Polyatomic ions, Level 2

Ammonium, NH₄⁺, is not a metal element. It behaves like a metal. Choose Polyatomic, like a metal.

Nonmetal groups: nitrate NO₃⁻, sulfate SO₄²⁻, hydroxide OH⁻, carbonate CO₃²⁻, and phosphate PO₄³⁻. Choose Polyatomic, nonmetal group.

Then pick the bond

Ionic: a metal, or ammonium, with a nonmetal or a nonmetal group.

Covalent: every part is a nonmetal or a nonmetal group. HCl, HNO₃, and H₂SO₄ are covalent. They can form ions in water, but the compound itself is molecular.

How an answer works

Step 1: each element is a metal or a nonmetal. Hydrogen is a nonmetal.

Step 2: a metal with a nonmetal is ionic. Two nonmetals are covalent.

Level 2 adds polyatomic ions. Ammonium, NH₄⁺, acts like a metal. Nitrate, sulfate, hydroxide, carbonate, and phosphate are nonmetal groups.
